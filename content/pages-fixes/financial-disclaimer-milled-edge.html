What this page covers. This page explains the publication’s scope, how examples are prepared, and where readers can check sources and assumptions.

Milled Edge publishes general education about household finance, investing, business calculations and market context. It does not assess an individual reader’s objectives, obligations or circumstances.

Nothing on this site is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security, product or scheme. Nothing on it is investment, tax, legal or accounting advice for a specific person. A calculator on these pages runs arithmetic on the numbers you type; it does not know whether the decision behind those numbers is a good one.

Checks for informed decisions

Promises“Guaranteed returns.”

A headline promising fixed returns may omit risks, fees or conditions. Check the issuer, product documents and applicable protections before relying on such a claim.

A genuine edge rarely needs to be whispered in a direct message, and never needs your account credentials.

03Accuracy and dates

Sources and dates

Milled Edge aims to check factual claims against reliable sources and cite key figures where available. Pages carry review dates where provided. The further a page is from today, the more its numbers may have moved — tax slabs, contribution limits, policy rates and provider rules all change quietly.

When something is wrong, it is corrected openly: a dated note is added at the end of the page rather than the sentence being quietly replaced. A verified error is corrected as capacity allows.

Where an external feed supplies a price or a rate, the page says so and shows the timestamp. A delayed quote is not an executable price, and a cached figure is not a promise.

✦The method

How figures are prepared

  1. Source itAn official release, a filing, a primary dataset. Where none exists, the page says what it is working from and what it assumes.
  2. Model itThe arithmetic is written down, the assumptions are named, and the result is recomputed before it is published.
  3. Show itThe working is printed with the figure, so a reader can disagree with a specific step rather than with the whole page.
  4. Date itPages show a review date where one is available; figures and rules can still change after publication.

04Market risk

Weighing risk and return

An informed choice considers how an investment might perform and how it could lose value. Past performance says little about what happens next; a strategy that worked in one decade may fail in another.

  • Borrowing to invest can magnify losses and leave an obligation even if the investment falls. Consider whether a loss could affect essential expenses; this page cannot assess your circumstances.
  • Diversification, horizon and behaviour usually matter more than any single choice.
  • An illustration that assumes a smooth return is not a model of any real market path.
  • Before acting, set out the downside first — if it is unaffordable, the upside is irrelevant.

✦Three myths

Common misconceptions

the desk
“A finance writer's word is advice.”
Even this one's is not. Real advice needs your documents on the table, a named professional taking responsibility, and a signature at the end of it.
“Great returns come from secret tips.”
They mostly come from time in the market, patience, low costs and avoiding expensive disasters. These are general principles, not guarantees or substitutes for assessing a specific investment.
“Read a page once and it stays true.”
Numbers drift and rules change silently. Revisit the page, recheck the source, and only then decide.
