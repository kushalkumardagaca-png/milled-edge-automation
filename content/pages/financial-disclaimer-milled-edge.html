01Purpose

What this publication is for.

Why this page exists at all. A publication that shows its arithmetic is also a publication that can be checked — so it has to say exactly what it is claiming, and what it is not.

Milled Edge publishes research-led writing about money: household finance, investing, business arithmetic and the market context around them. Every page is prepared with care and is general in nature. It cannot consider your objectives, your obligations or your situation, because none of those are known to it.

Nothing on this site is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security, product or scheme. Nothing on it is investment, tax, legal or accounting advice for a specific person. A calculator on these pages runs arithmetic on the numbers you type; it does not know whether the decision behind those numbers is a good one.

The map is drawn carefully. The driving is yours, and so is the vehicle.why every page opens with the working

These boundaries apply to the whole site — its pages, its tools, its correspondence and its social accounts. By reading on, you accept them.

✦The boundary, in two columns

This, and not this.

The distinction a reader most often gets wrong, stated once and plainly.

This is

  • General money education, written to be understood
  • Modelled illustrations with the assumptions printed beside them
  • Reasoning you can check line by line, and correct if it is wrong
  • Journalism about markets and policy, with sources named

This is not

  • Personal investment, tax or legal advice
  • Stock tips, calls or recommendations
  • An offer to buy or sell anything
  • A substitute for an adviser who can see your whole position

Your money moves only on your own signature. No page here, and no reply to an email, changes that.

02No personal advice

Reading this creates no adviser relationship.

Where the boundary came from

Three versions of the truth, held up together.

The boy doing the listening lived one of those versions: middle-class arithmetic, school fees, a loan, a salary that arrives and leaves. Later came the other side — ultra-high-net-worth clients whose decisions move numbers no household ever sees — and, in between, the government’s own view, the one that quietly sets the terms both sides play by.

Holding all three up together is what makes this publication careful. It is also why it never pretends to know your file: it has seen enough of them to know how different they are.

The publication is written by a finance writer. It is not a registered investment adviser, a broker, an audit firm or an accounting practice, and it does not hold client money or manage portfolios. Reading a page, subscribing, commenting or writing to the desk creates no professional, fiduciary or advisory relationship of any kind.

  • Tax rules, interest rates and product features change — often without announcement.
  • Worked examples use illustrative numbers, not predictions of your outcome.
  • What suits one balance sheet can be entirely wrong for the next one.
  • A reply to an email is a letter, not an engagement.
If a decision is large enough to matter, it is large enough to pay a professional for. A qualified adviser, accountant or lawyer can see the documents this site never will — and can be held accountable for the answer.

✦Red flags

Four things to distrust, here or anywhere.

Promises“Guaranteed returns.”

Markets deal in probabilities. A guarantee is either a misunderstanding or a sales tactic — there is no third option.

Pressure“This week only.”

Urgency is a marketing condition, not a market condition. A real opportunity survives a night's sleep.

Secrecy“Ask me privately.”

A genuine edge rarely needs to be whispered in a direct message, and never needs your account credentials.

CredentialsUnverifiable advisers.

Check the registration before you check the track record. The order matters.

03Accuracy and dates

Every figure has a date, because numbers drift.

Facts are checked against sources believed reliable at the time of publication, and the source is named beside the figure. Pages carry a review date. The further a page is from today, the more its numbers may have moved — tax slabs, contribution limits, policy rates and provider rules all change quietly.

When something is wrong, it is corrected openly: a dated note is added at the end of the page rather than the sentence being quietly replaced. A verified error is corrected within two working days of being reported.

Where an external feed supplies a price or a rate, the page says so and shows the timestamp. A delayed quote is not an executable price, and a cached figure is not a promise.

✦The method

How a number gets onto a page.

  1. Source itAn official release, a filing, a primary dataset. Where none exists, the page says what it is working from and what it assumes.
  2. Model itThe arithmetic is written down, the assumptions are named, and the result is recomputed before it is published.
  3. Show itThe working is printed with the figure, so a reader can disagree with a specific step rather than with the whole page.
  4. Date itEvery page carries the date it was last reviewed, so an old number is never mistaken for a current one.

04Market risk

Investing can lose money. That sentence is the whole clause.

Any investment can fall in value, including every one discussed on this site. Past performance says very little about what happens next, and a strategy that worked in one decade can fail in the next without anyone having made an obvious mistake.

  • Never invest money you have borrowed, and never invest an amount whose loss would change how you live.
  • Diversification, horizon and behaviour usually matter more than any single choice.
  • An illustration that assumes a smooth return is not a model of any real market path.
  • Before acting, set out the downside first — if it is unaffordable, the upside is irrelevant.

✦Three myths

Three beliefs worth flipping over.

the desk
“A finance writer's word is advice.”
Even this one's is not. Real advice needs your documents on the table, a named professional taking responsibility, and a signature at the end of it.
“Great returns come from secret tips.”
They mostly come from time in the market, patience, low costs and avoiding expensive disasters. None of those is secret, which is why none of them is sold.
“Read a page once and it stays true.”
Numbers drift and rules change silently. Revisit the page, recheck the source, and only then decide.

05Independence

No paid reviews, no paid links.

Editorial conclusions here are not for sale. If a sponsored or commissioned piece ever appears, it is disclosed at the top of the piece in plain words, and the verdict stays independent — a page can be commissioned, but never its conclusion.

Affiliate links, where they exist, are marked as such. Advertising that runs alongside the site is served by Google on the site owner’s account, is clearly labelled, and never influences what a page concludes.

  • No pre-written guest articles, and no paid placements.
  • No payment for a rating, an endorsement or a mention.
  • Disagreeing with an advertiser is not a conflict here; it is the arrangement working.

06External links

A citation is not an endorsement.

Pages link out to data sources, official documents, tools and video, and some embed charts from third-party providers. Those sites run under their own terms and their own privacy practices, and their content can change after a link is published.

A link means the destination was relevant at the time it was cited. It is not a promise about what that site publishes today, and it is not an endorsement of everything it does tomorrow.

✦If something is wrong

How a correction actually happens.

  1. You send the linkThe page address and the sentence or figure that looks wrong — a public source speeds it up more than a strong opinion does.
  2. It is checkedAgainst the source named on the page, and against the current version of the rule or figure it describes.
  3. It is fixed in publicA dated note is added at the end of the page. Nothing is quietly replaced, so a reader who quoted the old figure can see why it changed.
